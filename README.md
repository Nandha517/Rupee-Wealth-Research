# Rupee-Wealth-Research
Historical analysis of Indian equities, US equities in INR, gold, currency depreciation and inflation, 2016–2026.

# The ₹100 Dollar Question
### Does a falling rupee change which investments build wealth?

**A historical investment research project | January 2016 – September 2026**

## 1. Project Overview

How does currency depreciation influence investment returns for an Indian investor? Does investing in US equities provide a different wealth-building outcome when returns are converted into Indian rupees? How do gold, Indian equities, currency movements and inflation interact over time?

This project examines these questions by comparing the historical performance of Indian equities, US equities measured in INR, and gold. It combines investment return analysis, currency conversion, drawdown analysis, inflation considerations and scenario-based modelling in Microsoft Excel.

## 2. Research Objectives

- Compare historical investment performance using total returns and compound annual growth rates (CAGR).
- Examine how USD/INR movements influence the INR-denominated return of US equities.
- Compare historical peak-to-trough drawdowns.
- Distinguish nominal investment growth from inflation-adjusted purchasing power.
- Explore alternative investment outcomes using Excel Scenario Manager.

## 4. Key Historical Findings

The supplied dataset indicates that the INR-converted S&P 500 proxy and source INR gold series recorded higher annualised nominal returns than Nifty 50 TRI during the selected period.

Nifty 50 TRI recorded the largest reported monthly peak-to-trough drawdown among the three series. These findings highlight why investment comparisons should consider returns, downside movements, currency effects and inflation together.

The findings are historical and should not be interpreted as forecasts or investment recommendations.

## 5. Methodology

The analysis uses monthly observations from January 2016 to September 2026.

- **Total return:** Calculated from the first and last available observations.
- **CAGR:** Measures annualised growth over the selected period.
- **Drawdown:** Measures the decline from a previous observed peak.
- **Currency conversion:** Converts USD-denominated equity values into INR using the selected USD/INR series.
- **Inflation adjustment:** Uses the CPI series to assess changes in purchasing power.
- **Scenario analysis:** Uses alternative return, exchange-rate and inflation assumptions to illustrate hypothetical future outcomes.

## 8. Limitations

The analysis depends on the accuracy, timing, units and consistency of the supplied data. The S&P 500 INR series may be an approximation when monthly-average exchange rates are used. The index's total-return status and the CPI series must be verified before final publication.

The analysis does not fully account for taxes, transaction costs, fund expenses, tracking error or investor-specific circumstances. Scenario outputs are hypothetical sensitivity tests, not predictions.

## 9. Conclusion

The project demonstrates that an Indian investor's assessment of wealth creation can change when currency movements and inflation are considered alongside investment returns. Comparing Indian equities, US equities converted into INR and gold provides a framework for understanding these differences over a common historical period.
