# Rupee-Wealth-Research
Historical analysis of Indian equities, US equities in INR, gold, currency depreciation and inflation, 2016–2026.

# The ₹100 Dollar Question

### Does a falling rupee change which investments build wealth?

## Project Overview

This research compares the historical performance of Indian equities, US equities measured in Indian rupees, and gold over January 2016–September 2026. It examines how currency movements and inflation affect the interpretation of investment returns.

## Research Objectives

- Compare historical total returns and compound annual growth rates (CAGR).
- Examine the role of USD/INR movements in US equity returns for an Indian investor.
- Compare observed drawdowns across the selected investment series.
- Distinguish nominal returns from inflation-adjusted outcomes.
- Use Excel Scenario Manager to explore alternative future assumptions.

## Assets and Indicators

- Nifty 50 Total Return Index (TRI)
- S&P 500 total-return series, subject to source verification
- Gold prices denominated in INR
- USD/INR exchange rate
- Indian Consumer Price Index (CPI)

## Key Historical Findings

In the supplied dataset, the S&P 500 INR proxy and source INR gold series recorded higher annualised nominal returns than Nifty 50 TRI. Nifty 50 TRI showed the largest reported monthly peak-to-trough drawdown among the three series.

These are historical findings for the selected period, not predictions of future performance.

## Methodology

Returns are calculated from monthly observations. CAGR measures annualised growth between the first and last observations. Drawdown measures the decline from a previous peak. Currency conversion and CPI adjustments are used to examine returns from an Indian investor's perspective.

## Limitations

The findings depend on source accuracy, index definitions, exchange-rate timing and CPI consistency. The INR-converted S&P 500 series is a proxy when monthly-average exchange rates are used. The analysis excludes transaction costs, taxes, fund expenses and other investor-specific costs. Scenario outputs are hypothetical, not forecasts.
